## 0. Purpose of the chapter, and the split with its neighbours

### 0.1 Purpose

The paper works prime by prime. At each bad prime $q$ it reads off a handful of local invariants of $E$: the
Kodaira type, the geometric component group $\Phig_q$ and the Tamagawa number $c_q$, $v_q(\Delta)$ and $v_q(j)$, the
conductor exponent, the Tate parameter $q_E$ and its unit part $u$ at a potentially multiplicative prime, the
order of the image of inertia at a potentially good prime (Kraus), and the effect on all of these of the quadratic
twist by $\Q(\sqrt{q^*})$. This chapter defines every one of these objects, proves what can be proved at the
book's level, states the rest precisely, and checks all of it on the paper's curves. Concretely it must deliver:

1. **Definition def:terms.** $v_q(j_E)$ as an invariant of the curve (not the model), the trichotomy good,
   multiplicative, additive, and the dichotomy potentially good ($v_q(j)\ge0$) versus potentially multiplicative
   ($v_q(j)<0$); the Tate parameter $q_E$ with $v_q(q_E)=-v_q(j)$; the statement that $t_\ell(E,q)=v_\ell(-v_q(j))$ is
   $v_\ell(n)$ for $\mathrm I_n$ at every $q$ and for $\mathrm I_n^*$ at odd $q$, while at $q=2$ a symbol $\mathrm I_m^*$ is either potentially
   good or has $m=\nu+4$ or $m=\nu+8$ with $\nu=-v_2(j)$ (items 7.4, 7.7, 9.4).
2. **The table of Section 1 and Lemma lem:euler (the local half).** For $q\ge5$ the Kodaira type is read off
   $v_q(\Delta_{\min})$ and $v_q(c_4)$ (item 5.3); the image of inertia at a potentially good prime has order
   $12/\gcd(v_q(\Delta),12)$ (Kraus, item 6.6). The Euler factors themselves are Chapter 19's.
3. **Section 1.4 of the paper.** Neron models, $\Phig_q=\Phi_q(\bar\F_q)$ and its order and structure for each type,
   $c_q=\#\Phi_q(\F_q)$ and why $c_q\ne\#\Phig_q$ in general; the exchange of types under the twist by $\Q(\sqrt{q^*})$; the
   weight $w_\ell(E,q)$ and the formula $w_\ell(E,q)=\max(v_\ell\#\Phig_q(E),v_\ell\#\Phig_q(E^{(q)}))$ (items 4.1 to 4.4, 5.1, 8.4).
4. **Lemma lem:twist (i), (ii), proved in full** (Chapter 9 supplied the models and invariants of twists; here the
   types, component groups and conductor exponents), with important_notes.md points 28, 29, 45 built in (items 8.1, 8.2).
5. **Lemma lem:tamagawa-exp and Lemma lem:tamfactor (the curve-theoretic input).** The Tate curve, Tate's
   uniformisation theorem, the fact that a split multiplicative curve is a Tate curve and a nonsplit one becomes
   one over the unramified quadratic extension, $E_{q}[\ell^t]$ as an extension of $\Z/\ell^t$ by $\mu_{\ell^t}$ generated by an
   $\ell^t$-th root of $q$, and the unit part $u$ (items 7.1 to 7.5). The Galois-representation consequences are Chapter 11's.
6. **Lemma lem:IV and Remark rem:types (the curve-theoretic input).** Kraus's theorem on the semistability defect
   (item 6.6), and the Serre-Tate description of the minimal extension of good reduction (item 6.5). Chapter 11
   turns them into statements about $\rho(I_q)$ and $E[3]^{I_q}$.
7. **Remark rem:wild, Section sec:wild and Proposition prop:forms.** What changes at $2$ and $3$: wild conductor
   exponents ($f_2\le8$, $f_3\le5$), the classification of inertia images at $2$, the theorem that types $\mathrm{IV}$, $\mathrm{IV}^*$
   at $2$ are exactly the tame additive potentially good curves at $2$ and have inertia image cyclic of order $3$
   (item 6.8: this settles important_notes.md point 43 and removes the exception in Proposition prop:forms), and the
   facts at $3$ (types $\mathrm{II}$, $\mathrm{II}^*$, $\mathrm{IV}$, $\mathrm{IV}^*$ are always wild there) (items 6.7, 6.8, 9.1 to 9.4).
8. **The appendix of the paper.** PARI's Kodaira codes and the outputs of `ellglobalred` and `elllocalred` (item 5.5,
   10.2), Cremona's tables (10.1).
9. **Discharging deferrals.** Chapter 9 sends here: minimality and its criterion, reduction types, $a_p\in\{1,-1,0\}$ at
   bad primes, the reduction map and its injectivity on prime-to-$p$ torsion (needed for Chapter 9's items 8.6 and
   9.2), the Neron differential, Kodaira types, the conductor, the formal group, the Tate curve, Lemma lem:twist (i),
   (ii), and the invariance of $v_\ell(-v_q(j))$ under isogenies of degree prime to $\ell$ (its 9.9(iii)). Chapter 7 sends:
   the Tate curve itself, Kraus's theorem, the Kodaira column of its table 9.4 and the hypothesis of its 9.5(ii)
   (cyclic inertia of order $3$ for type $\mathrm{IV}$ at $2$). Chapter 1 sends: component groups as finite abelian groups.
   Chapter 2 sends: the unit part of the Tate parameter. Chapter 5 sends: $E_q[\ell^t]$ generated by $\mu_{\ell^t}$ and an
   $\ell^t$-th root of $q$. Chapter 6 sends: the twist by $\Q(\sqrt{q^*})$ is unramified outside $q$ (used in 8.1). Chapter 8
   sends: smoothness and singular points of reductions. Chapter 16 sends: $c_q=n$ (split), $1$ or $2$ (nonsplit), and
   the unit part of the Tate parameter of 11a1 at $11$. All are delivered (Part 5).

## 1. Global conventions fixed in this chapter

* **R1 (local setting).** $K$ is a field complete for a discrete valuation $v$, of characteristic $0$, with valuation ring
  $\mathcal O$, maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=\pi\mathcal O$, perfect residue field $k$ of characteristic $p$, and $v(\pi)=1$. In every application $K$ is
  $\Q_p$, a finite extension of $\Q_p$, or the completion of the maximal unramified extension $\Q_p^{\mathrm{ur}}$ (residue field
  $\bar\F_p$); Chapter 7, conventions L1, L2. When the paper's prime is meant, $K=\Q_q$ and $p=q$. The letter $q$ is also the Tate
  parameter's variable; the chapter writes the Tate curve as $E_{\mathsf q}$ or $E_t$ with a different letter where both
  occur in one display (writer's choice, fixed once), and calls the Tate parameter of $E$ always $q_E$ (important_notes.md
  point 8).
* **R2 (models).** "Model" means a Weierstrass equation with the general $a_1,\dots,a_6$ (Chapter 9, C3), never only a short
  one: at $p=2,3$ short models are never minimal in general. Coordinate changes $(u,r,s,t)$ and their effect on
  $c_4,c_6,\Delta$ are Chapter 9's item 1.3.
* **R3 (types and codes).** Kodaira symbols as in PLAN.md 7.1. PARI's codes (item 5.5) appear only in tables, in
  `lstlisting` and in `paperlink` environments.
* **R4 (two component groups).** $\Phig_p=\Phi_p(\bar k)$ is the geometric component group (the paper's $\Phig_q$); $c_p=\#\Phi_p(k)$ is
  the Tamagawa number. Never write $c_p$ for $\#\Phig_p$; never use $c$ for the class of the unit part of the Tate
  parameter (write $c(u)$; important_notes.md point 23).
* **R5 (conductor exponent).** $f_p=f_p(E)$; the letter $\delta_p$ is reserved for the paper's $\dim V^{I_p}$ (Section 1.1), so the
  wild part is written $\operatorname{sw}_p(E)$ (Swan conductor), as in Chapter 7.
* **R6 (semistability defect).** For potentially good reduction $\Phi_K$ (or $\Phi_p$ over $\Q_p$) denotes $\Gal(L/K^{\mathrm{ur}})$ for the minimal
  extension $L$ of good reduction (item 6.5). To avoid a clash with the component group $\Phi_p$, the writer must name it
  differently in the chapter: **$G_{\mathrm{ss}}$ (semistability group)** is recommended, with "defect" $e=\#G_{\mathrm{ss}}$; this outline
  writes $G_{\mathrm{ss}}$.
* **R7 (Frobenius).** As in Chapter 7 (L3): $\Frob$ is arithmetic in group-theoretic statements. Chapter 10 needs Frobenius
  only in item 2.2 (split versus nonsplit) and in the `paperlink` of item 6.8.
* **R8 (characters).** $q^*=(-1)^{(q-1)/2}q$ and $\chi_q=\chi_{q^*}$, the quadratic character of conductor $q$ (Chapter 2, C5; Chapter 9, C4). The
  paper's $E^{(q)}$ is the twist by $q^*$, not by $q$ (point 29).
* **R9 (typography).** PLAN.md Section 7.2: no dash ligatures (write "Kodaira-Neron", "Neron-Ogg-Shafarevich",
  "Ogg-Saito", "Dokchitser-Dokchitser", ranges "1 to 11"); ASCII source; labels `...:reduction:...`. `preamble.tex`
  does not load TikZ (checked), and chapters may not add packages: the special fibres are therefore given as a
  `booktabs` table (components, multiplicities, intersection pattern in words) and, if the writer wishes, as small
  LaTeX `picture` environments (no package needed). Bibliography keys: the paper's and `refs.bib`'s (`Tate75` is Tate's
  algorithm; PLAN.md's list also names `TateAlgorithm`, which is not in `refs.bib`: use `Tate75`).
