Conventions fixed for the whole chapter (and for Chapters 17, 18, which inherit them):

* $\ell$ is a prime; where "$\ell$ odd" is needed it is said explicitly (it is needed in the model computation, where $2$ must be a unit,
  and in the vanishing of the cohomology of $\mathbb{Z}/2$).
* All cohomology of a profinite group is continuous cochain cohomology. Coefficient modules are of three types: (D) discrete
  modules (finite, or $\ell$-primary torsion such as $W=T\otimes\mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell$); (C) finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-modules with the
  $\ell$-adic topology and a continuous action; (V) finite-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-vector spaces with a continuous action.
* Frobenius is always the arithmetic Frobenius ($x\mapsto x^q$ on $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q$); $G_{\mathbb{Q}_q}/I_q=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q/\mathbb{F}_q)\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$
  is topologically generated by it; $\chi_\ell(\mathrm{Frob}_q)=q$ on $\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)=\varprojlim\mu_{\ell^k}$ for $q\ne\ell$.
* The conjugation action of $G/H$ on $H^n(H,M)$ is $(\sigma\cdot c)(h)=\sigma\,c(\sigma^{-1}h\sigma)$, exactly the paper's formula.
* The relation between Frobenius and a topological generator $t$ of the pro-$\ell$ tame inertia is
  $\mathrm{Frob}\,t\,\mathrm{Frob}^{-1}=t^{q}$, equivalently $\mathrm{Frob}^{-1}t\,\mathrm{Frob}=t^{q^{-1}}$ with $q^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_\ell$
  (proved in Chapter 7; imported here as a stated fact).
* $\mathrm{ad}^0T$ (the paper's `\ad T`) is the module of trace-zero endomorphisms of $T$ with $g\cdot\varphi=\rho(g)\varphi\rho(g)^{-1}$.

## 1. Core results, in teaching order

Format of each entry: **name** (kind). Statement. *Rigour.* Reference. Paper use.

### Section 2: complexes and the long exact sequence

**cochain-complex** (definition). A cochain complex of $R$-modules ($R$ commutative, in practice $\mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$) is a sequence
$C^\bullet=(C^n,d^n\colon C^n\to C^{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}$ with $d^{n+1}d^n=0$. Cocycles $Z^n=\ker d^n$, coboundaries $B^n=\operatorname{im}d^{n-1}\subseteq Z^n$,
cohomology $H^n(C^\bullet)=Z^n/B^n$. A morphism $f\colon C^\bullet\to D^\bullet$ is a family $f^n$ with $df^n=f^{n+1}d$; it induces $H^n(f)$, functorially.
$f,g$ are homotopic if $f^n-g^n=ds^n+s^{n+1}d$ for maps $s^n\colon C^n\to D^{n-1}$; homotopic morphisms induce the same map on cohomology.
A sequence of complexes is exact if it is exact in each degree.
*Proved here.* Weibel, Chapter 1, Sections 1.1 to 1.4. Background (all long exact sequences of Chapters 17 and 18).

**snake-lemma** (lemma). Given a commutative diagram with exact rows $A'\to A\to A''\to0$ and $0\to B'\to B\to B''$ and vertical maps
$f',f,f''$, the sequence $\ker f'\to\ker f\to\ker f''\xrightarrow{\delta}\operatorname{coker}f'\to\operatorname{coker}f\to\operatorname{coker}f''$ is exact, where
$\delta(a'')$ is defined by lifting $a''\in\ker f''$ to $a\in A$, observing that $f(a)\mapsto0$ in $B''$ so $f(a)$ is the image of a unique $b'\in B'$,
and putting $\delta(a'')=[b']$; $\delta$ is well defined and $R$-linear. If $A'\to A$ is injective the sequence begins with $0\to\ker f'$; if
$B\to B''$ is surjective it ends with $\operatorname{coker}f''\to0$.
*Stated; reference (proved in Chapter 3); the construction of $\delta$ is repeated here because it is the connecting map of everything that follows.*
Weibel, Lemma 1.3.2. Background.

**long-exact-sequence** (theorem). Let $0\to A^\bullet\xrightarrow{i}B^\bullet\xrightarrow{p}C^\bullet\to0$ be a short exact sequence of cochain complexes.
For each $n$ there is a connecting homomorphism $\delta^n\colon H^n(C^\bullet)\to H^{n+1}(A^\bullet)$: represent a class by $c\in Z^n(C)$, lift to $b\in B^n$,
then $p(db)=dc=0$ so $db=i(a)$ for a unique $a\in A^{n+1}$, which is a cocycle, and $\delta^n[c]=[a]$. This is well defined, and
$\cdots\to H^n(A)\xrightarrow{i_*}H^n(B)\xrightarrow{p_*}H^n(C)\xrightarrow{\delta^n}H^{n+1}(A)\xrightarrow{i_*}H^{n+1}(B)\to\cdots$ is exact.
Naturality: a morphism of short exact sequences of complexes induces a morphism of long exact sequences (commuting ladder).
*Proved here.* Weibel, Theorem 1.3.1. Background (Kummer sequences of Chapter 17, the sequence of Lemma lem:dfg21).

### Section 3: resolutions, derived functors, Ext and Tor (outline)

**projective-injective-resolutions** (definition). $P$ is projective if $\operatorname{Hom}_R(P,-)$ is exact (equivalently a direct summand of a free module);
$I$ is injective if $\operatorname{Hom}_R(-,I)$ is exact. Every module $M$ has a projective resolution $\cdots\to P_1\to P_0\to M\to0$ (free modules suffice)
and an injective resolution $0\to M\to I^0\to I^1\to\cdots$ ($R$-Mod has enough injectives). Comparison theorem: two projective (resp. injective)
resolutions of $M$ are homotopy equivalent, and a map $M\to N$ lifts to a map of resolutions, unique up to homotopy. Over a PID (so over
$\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$) every module has a projective resolution of length one, and divisible modules such as $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ are injective.
*Stated; reference.* Weibel, Sections 2.2 and 2.3. Background.

**ext-tor** (definition). $\operatorname{Ext}^n_R(M,N)=H^n(\operatorname{Hom}_R(P_\bullet,N))=H^n(\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,I^\bullet))$ for a projective resolution $P_\bullet\to M$ or an
injective resolution $N\to I^\bullet$ (the two agree and are independent of choices); $\operatorname{Tor}^R_n(M,N)=H_n(P_\bullet\otimes_RN)$.
$\operatorname{Ext}^0=\operatorname{Hom}$, $\operatorname{Tor}_0=\otimes$; a short exact sequence in either variable gives a long exact sequence;
$\operatorname{Ext}^n(M,N)=0$ for $n\ge1$ if $M$ is projective or $N$ injective; over a PID $\operatorname{Ext}^n=\operatorname{Tor}_n=0$ for $n\ge2$.
Examples: $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{Z}/m,N)\simeq N/mN$, $\operatorname{Tor}^{\mathbb{Z}}_1(\mathbb{Z}/m,N)\simeq N[m]$, $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^a,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^b)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{\min(a,b)}$.
*Stated; reference.* Weibel, Sections 2.4 to 2.7 and 3.1 to 3.3. Background.

**ext1-extensions** (theorem). $\operatorname{Ext}^1_R(M,N)$ is in bijection with the equivalence classes of extensions $0\to N\to E\to M\to0$ of $R$-modules
(equivalence: an isomorphism $E\to E'$ inducing the identity on $N$ and on $M$); $0$ corresponds to the split extension $N\oplus M$; the group law is the Baer sum.
*Stated; reference.* Weibel, Theorem 3.4.3. Background (the cocycle in the corner of a reducible representation, below).

### Section 4: group cohomology by cochains

**g-module** (definition). For a group $G$, a $G$-module is an abelian group $M$ with a left action by automorphisms, i.e. a left $\mathbb{Z}[G]$-module.
Invariants $M^G$; coinvariants $M_G=M/I_GM$ with $I_G$ the augmentation ideal (generated by the $g-1$); for finite $G$ the norm $N_G=\sum_gg$,
with $N_GM\subseteq M^G$. Trivial modules. $\operatorname{Hom}_G(M,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,N)^G$ for $(g\varphi)(m)=g\varphi(g^{-1}m)$. If $M$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module and $G$
acts $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-linearly, every cohomology group below is a $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module and every map is $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-linear.
*Proved here.* Brown, Chapter I; Serre, Local Fields, Chapter VII, Section 1. Background.

**inhomogeneous-cochains** (definition). $C^n(G,M)=\operatorname{Map}(G^n,M)$ ($C^0=M$), with
$(d^n\varphi)(g_1,\dots,g_{n+1})=g_1\varphi(g_2,\dots,g_{n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^n(-1)^i\varphi(g_1,\dots,g_ig_{i+1},\dots,g_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}\varphi(g_1,\dots,g_n)$.
Then $d^{n+1}d^n=0$ and $H^n(G,M):=H^n(C^\bullet(G,M))$. Low degrees: $(d^0m)(g)=gm-m$; $(d^1\varphi)(g,h)=g\varphi(h)-\varphi(gh)+\varphi(g)$;
$(d^2\psi)(g,h,k)=g\psi(h,k)-\psi(gh,k)+\psi(g,hk)-\psi(g,h)$. $H^n(G,-)$ is a covariant functor.
*Proved here ($d\circ d=0$ checked in degrees $\le2$ in the text; general degree by reference).* Brown, Chapter III, Section 1; Serre, Local Fields, Chapter VII,
Section 3; NSW, Chapter I, Section 2. Every paper hook of the chapter.

**cohomology-as-ext** (theorem). $H^n(G,M)\simeq\operatorname{Ext}^n_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(\mathbb{Z},M)$ ($\mathbb{Z}$ with trivial action): the inhomogeneous cochain complex is
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}[G]}(F_\bullet,M)$ for the bar resolution $F_\bullet$ of $\mathbb{Z}$ by free $\mathbb{Z}[G]$-modules ($F_n$ free on $G^n$). So $H^n(G,-)$ is the $n$-th right
derived functor of $M\mapsto M^G$ and any projective resolution of $\mathbb{Z}$ over $\mathbb{Z}[G]$ computes it.
*Stated; reference.* Brown, Chapter I, Section 5 and Chapter III, Section 1; Weibel, Section 6.5. Background.

**h0-h1** (proposition). $H^0(G,M)=M^G$. $H^1(G,M)=Z^1/B^1$ with $Z^1=\{\varphi\colon G\to M:\varphi(gh)=\varphi(g)+g\varphi(h)\}$ (crossed homomorphisms)
and $B^1=\{g\mapsto gm-m\}$ (principal ones). A $1$-cocycle has $\varphi(1)=0$ and $\varphi(g^{-1})=-g^{-1}\varphi(g)$. If $G$ acts trivially, $B^1=0$ and
$H^1(G,M)=\operatorname{Hom}(G,M)=\operatorname{Hom}(G^{\mathrm{ab}},M)$; in particular $H^1(G,M)=0$ if $G$ is finite and $M$ is torsion-free with trivial action.
For $0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0$ exact, $0\to M'^G\to M^G\to M''^G\xrightarrow{\delta}H^1(G,M')\to H^1(G,M)\to H^1(G,M'')$ is exact with
$\delta(m'')=[g\mapsto gm-m]$ for any lift $m$ of $m''$.
*Proved here.* Serre, Local Fields, Chapter VII, Section 3; Brown, Chapter III, Section 1. Lemma lem:tamfactor ($\kappa$ is a $1$-cocycle; the Kummer
class in $H^1(G,\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1))$), Lemma lem:dfg21, Lemma lem:h0 ($H^0$ of $W^D$).

**cocycle-in-the-corner** (proposition / worked example). Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $\chi_1,\chi_2\colon G\to R^\times$ characters and
$\rho\colon G\to\operatorname{GL}_2(R)$ of the form $\rho(g)=\begin{pmatrix}\chi_1(g)&\kappa(g)\\0&\chi_2(g)\end{pmatrix}$ (so $Re_1$ is stable, with character
$\chi_1$, and the quotient has character $\chi_2$). Then $g\mapsto\kappa(g)\chi_2(g)^{-1}$ is a $1$-cocycle with values in $R(\chi_1\chi_2^{-1})$ ($R$ with $g$
acting by $\chi_1(g)\chi_2(g)^{-1}$); its class in $H^1(G,R(\chi_1\chi_2^{-1}))$ depends only on the isomorphism class of $(\rho,Re_1)$ (replacing $e_2$ by
$e_2+re_1$ changes the cocycle by $d(r)$), and the class is $0$ if and only if $\rho$ is split (there is a $G$-stable complement of $Re_1$). Every class arises,
so $H^1(G,R(\chi_1\chi_2^{-1}))=\operatorname{Ext}^1_{R[G]}(R(\chi_2),R(\chi_1))$ classifies extensions of $\chi_2$ by $\chi_1$. In the paper's shape
$\rho(g)=\begin{pmatrix}\chi_\ell(g)\varepsilon(g)&\kappa(g)\\0&\varepsilon(g)\end{pmatrix}$ with $\varepsilon^2=1$ the cocycle is $\varepsilon\kappa$ with values in $\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$,
and on any subgroup where $\varepsilon=1$ (inertia) it is $\kappa$ itself; its restriction to inertia is the homomorphism $i\mapsto\kappa(i)$.
*Proved here.* Elementary; compare Serre, Local Fields, Chapter VII, Section 3 and DDT, Theorem 3.1(e) for the shape. Lemma lem:tamfactor (the matrix
$\begin{psmallmatrix}\chi_\ell\varepsilon&\kappa\\0&\varepsilon\end{psmallmatrix}$, "$\kappa$ the Kummer class"), Lemma lem:old (a non-split extension of two
characters: Chapter 15 uses the uniqueness of the stable line).

**h2-extensions** (theorem). $H^2(G,M)$ is in bijection with the equivalence classes of group extensions $0\to M\to E\to G\to1$ inducing the given action of $G$
on $M$ by conjugation; $0$ corresponds to $M\rtimes G$; the cocycle of an extension with a set-theoretic section $s$, $s(1)=1$, is $\psi(g,h)=s(g)s(h)s(gh)^{-1}$.
*Proof sketched here.* Brown, Chapter IV, Section 3. Background.

**functoriality-in-M-and-les** (proposition). A $G$-map $f\colon M\to N$ induces $f_*\colon H^n(G,M)\to H^n(G,N)$. A short exact sequence
$0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0$ of $G$-modules (abstract $G$) gives an exact sequence of cochain complexes, hence a long exact sequence
$0\to H^0(G,M')\to H^0(G,M)\to H^0(G,M'')\xrightarrow{\delta}H^1(G,M')\to H^1(G,M)\to H^1(G,M'')\xrightarrow{\delta}H^2(G,M')\to\cdots$, natural in the sequence
and $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-linear when the modules are $\mathbb{Z}_\ell[G]$-modules. (Profinite version: les-continuous below.)
*Proved here.* Brown, Chapter III, Section 6; NSW, Chapter I, Section 3. Lemma lem:dfg21, Lemma lem:h0, Kummer sequences of Chapter 17.

**h0-and-torsion** (lemma). (a) For a $G$-module $W$ and $m\ge1$: $H^0(G,W[m])=H^0(G,W)[m]$. (b) For $T$ a $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module without $\ell$-torsion with
$\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-linear $G$-action and $W=T\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}\mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell$: $W[\ell^k]\simeq T/\ell^kT$ as $G$-modules ($t\otimes\ell^{-k}\mapsto t\bmod\ell^k$), hence
$H^0(G,W)[\ell^k]=H^0(G,T/\ell^kT)$ and $H^0(G,W)=\varinjlim_kH^0(G,T/\ell^kT)$. (c) A finite $\ell$-primary abelian group $A$ with $\#A[\ell]\le\ell$ is cyclic,
$A\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell^h$ with $h=\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}A=\max\{k:A[\ell^k]\text{ has an element of order }\ell^k\}$. (d) Consequently, if $H^0(G,W)$ is finite
and $H^0(G,T/\ell T)$ has order at most $\ell$, then $\operatorname{length}H^0(G,W)$ is the largest $k$ for which $H^0(G,T/\ell^kT)$ contains an element of exact
order $\ell^k$ (an invariant element of $T/\ell^kT$ not divisible by $\ell$).
*Proved here.* Elementary (lengths from Chapter 3). Lemma lem:h0 ("the $\ell$-torsion of $H^0(\mathbb{Q},W^D)$ is $H^0(\mathbb{Q},\operatorname{ad}^0\bar\rho\otimes\bar\chi_\ell)$";
"the description of the length follows from the $\ell^k$-torsion"), Conjecture conj:euler ($h_\ell(E)$), Remark rem:ns.

### Section 5: changing the group

**compatible-pairs** (definition / proposition). For a homomorphism $f\colon G'\to G$, a $G$-module $M$, a $G'$-module $M'$ and $\varphi\colon M\to M'$ with
$\varphi(f(g')m)=g'\varphi(m)$ (a compatible pair), $\varphi\circ(-)\circ f^{\times n}$ maps cocycles to cocycles and coboundaries to coboundaries and induces
$(f,\varphi)^*\colon H^n(G,M)\to H^n(G',M')$, compatible with the long exact sequences; composition of pairs gives composition of maps.
*Proved here.* Brown, Chapter III, Section 8; NSW, Chapter I, Section 5; Serre, Local Fields, Chapter VII, Section 5. Background (res, inf, conjugation are instances).

**res-inf-cor** (definition / proposition). (a) Restriction: for $H\le G$ (closed, in the profinite case), $\operatorname{res}\colon H^n(G,M)\to H^n(H,M)$ is induced by
$(H\hookrightarrow G,\operatorname{id})$: restrict cochains. (b) Inflation: for $H\trianglelefteq G$, $\operatorname{inf}\colon H^n(G/H,M^H)\to H^n(G,M)$ is induced by $(G\to G/H,M^H\hookrightarrow M)$.
(c) Corestriction: for $[G:H]<\infty$ there are maps $\operatorname{cor}\colon H^n(H,M)\to H^n(G,M)$, compatible with long exact sequences, given in degree $0$ by the norm
$m\mapsto\sum_{g\in G/H}gm$ and in degree $1$, for left coset representatives $g_1,\dots,g_r$ and $gg_i=g_{\tau(i)}h_i(g)$ with $h_i(g)\in H$, by
$(\operatorname{cor}\varphi)(g)=\sum_ig_{\tau(i)}\varphi(h_i(g))$. (d) $\operatorname{cor}\circ\operatorname{res}=[G:H]$ on $H^n(G,M)$ for all $n$; for $H$ normal,
$\operatorname{res}\circ\operatorname{cor}=\sum_{\sigma\in G/H}\sigma_*$ on $H^n(H,M)$ (conjugation action below).
*Proved here for (a), (b) and for (c), (d) in degrees $0$ and $1$; general degree stated with reference.* Brown, Chapter III, Section 9; Serre, Local Fields, Chapter VII,
Sections 5 and 7; NSW, Chapter I, Section 5; Cassels-Frohlich, Chapter IV. Lemma lem:tamfactor (inflation-restriction), Lemma lem:dfg21 ($2$ kills $H^1(\mathbb{R},-)$).

**order-kills** (theorem). Let $G$ be finite of order $N$ and $M$ a $G$-module. Then $N\cdot H^n(G,M)=0$ for all $n\ge1$. For $n=1$ directly: if $\varphi\in Z^1$ and
$m=\sum_{g\in G}\varphi(g)$, then summing $\varphi(\sigma g)=\varphi(\sigma)+\sigma\varphi(g)$ over $g$ gives $m=N\varphi(\sigma)+\sigma m$, i.e. $N\varphi=d(-m)$.
Consequences: (i) if $N$ is invertible on $M$ then $H^n(G,M)=0$ for $n\ge1$; (ii) if $M$ is finitely generated as an abelian group then $H^n(G,M)$ is finite for
$n\ge1$; (iii) if $\ell\nmid N$ and $M$ is an $\ell$-primary torsion group or a $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module, then $H^n(G,M)=0$ for $n\ge1$ and $M^G$ is a direct summand of $M$
(projector $N^{-1}N_G$).
*Proved here ($n=1$ in full; $n\ge2$ via cor$\circ$res with $H=1$).* Brown, Chapter III, Section 10; Serre, Local Fields, Chapter VIII, Section 2.
Lemma lem:dfg21 and the paragraph after it ($H^1(\mathbb{R},T^D)$ killed by $2$).

**conjugation-action** (proposition). Let $H\trianglelefteq G$ and $M$ a $G$-module. For $\sigma\in G$ the pair $(h\mapsto\sigma^{-1}h\sigma,\ m\mapsto\sigma m)$ is
compatible and induces $\sigma_*\colon H^n(H,M)\to H^n(H,M)$; on cochains $(\sigma\cdot\varphi)(h_1,\dots,h_n)=\sigma\,\varphi(\sigma^{-1}h_1\sigma,\dots,\sigma^{-1}h_n\sigma)$.
This is a left action of $G$ on $H^n(H,M)$, trivial on $H$ (for $n=1$: $(h_0\cdot\varphi)-\varphi=d(\varphi(h_0))$), hence an action of $G/H$, and the map induced by a class
of $G/H$ does not depend on the representative. It is $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-linear when $M$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_\ell[G]$-module, so it preserves $H^n(H,M)_{\mathrm{tors}}$, and
$H^n(H,M)_{\mathrm{tors}}^{G/H}$ is defined. The restriction $H^n(G,M)\to H^n(H,M)$ lands in $H^n(H,M)^{G/H}$ (for $n=1$: $\sigma\cdot\operatorname{res}\varphi-\operatorname{res}\varphi=d(\varphi(\sigma))$).
All of this holds verbatim for profinite $G$, closed normal $H$, and continuous cochains with coefficients of type (D), (C) or (V).
*Proved here ($n=1$ in full, general $n$ on cochains).* Brown, Chapter III, Section 8; NSW, Chapter I, Section 5; Serre, Local Fields, Chapter VII, Section 5.
Lemma lem:tamfactor ("$G/I$ acts on $H^1(I,M)$ by $(\sigma\cdot c)(i)=\sigma c(\sigma^{-1}i\sigma)$"), Section 1.2 of the paper
($\operatorname{Tam}_p(M)=\operatorname{Fitt}(H^1(I_p,M)_{\mathrm{tor}})^{G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}}$), Lemma lem:fpr.

**inflation-restriction** (theorem). Let $H\trianglelefteq G$ and $M$ a $G$-module. (a) $0\to H^1(G/H,M^H)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{inf}}H^1(G,M)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{res}}H^1(H,M)^{G/H}$
is exact. (b) (five-term exact sequence of Hochschild and Serre) There is a transgression $\operatorname{tg}$ with
$0\to H^1(G/H,M^H)\to H^1(G,M)\to H^1(H,M)^{G/H}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{tg}}H^2(G/H,M^H)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{inf}}H^2(G,M)$ exact.
(c) If $H^i(H,M)=0$ for $1\le i\le n-1$ then $0\to H^n(G/H,M^H)\to H^n(G,M)\to H^n(H,M)$ is exact. (d) Corollary: if $H^1(H,M)=0$ then
$\operatorname{inf}\colon H^1(G/H,M^H)\to H^1(G,M)$ is an isomorphism; in particular if $H$ acts trivially on $M$ and $\operatorname{Hom}(H,M)=0$ then $H^1(G/H,M)\simeq H^1(G,M)$.
Valid for abstract groups; for profinite $G$, closed normal $H$ and $M$ of type (D); and for continuous cochains with $M$ of type (C) or (V).
*(a), (d) proved here; (b), (c) stated with reference, the transgression sketched.* Serre, Local Fields, Chapter VII, Section 6; NSW, Chapter I and Chapter II, Section 4;
Weibel, Section 6.8; Milne, Class Field Theory, Chapter II. Lemma lem:tamfactor ("by inflation-restriction $H^1(I,M')=H^1(\langle t\rangle,M')$"), Chapter 17
($H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}=\ker(H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,M)\to H^1(I_p,M))=H^1(G/I,M^I)$).

**hochschild-serre** (theorem). For $H\trianglelefteq G$ and a $G$-module $M$ there is a first-quadrant spectral sequence $E_2^{p,q}=H^p(G/H,H^q(H,M))\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(G,M)$,
with $G/H$ acting on $H^q(H,M)$ by conjugation; edge maps are inflation and restriction; its five-term exact sequence is inflation-restriction (b). Same for profinite $G$,
closed normal $H$, $M$ of type (D). Consequence: if $H^q(H,M)=0$ for all $q\ge1$ then $\operatorname{inf}\colon H^p(G/H,M^H)\to H^p(G,M)$ is an isomorphism for all $p$.
*Stated; reference.* Brown, Chapter VII, Section 6; Weibel, Theorem 6.8.2; NSW, Chapter II, Section 4. Background (only the five-term sequence is used).

### Section 6: finite groups

**cyclic-group-cohomology** (theorem). Let $G=\langle t\rangle$ be cyclic of order $m$, $N=1+t+\dots+t^{m-1}$, $M$ a $G$-module. Then $\varphi\mapsto\varphi(t)$
induces $H^1(G,M)\simeq\ker(N\colon M\to M)/(t-1)M$ (a cocycle is determined by $a=\varphi(t)$ via $\varphi(t^k)=(1+t+\dots+t^{k-1})a$, and consistency at $t^m=1$
is $Na=0$); $H^2(G,M)\simeq M^G/NM$; and $H^{n+2}(G,M)\simeq H^n(G,M)$ for $n\ge1$. In Tate's notation $\hat H^0=M^G/NM$, $\hat H^{-1}=\ker N/(t-1)M$, and
$\hat H^n\simeq\hat H^{n+2}$ for all $n\in\mathbb{Z}$. Examples: trivial action on $\mathbb{Z}$: $H^1=0$, $H^2=\mathbb{Z}/m$; trivial action on $\mathbb{Z}/m$: $H^1=H^2=\mathbb{Z}/m$.
*$H^1$ proved here; $H^2$ and periodicity stated with reference.* Serre, Local Fields, Chapter VIII, Section 4; Cassels-Frohlich, Chapter IV, Section 8; Brown, Chapter III,
Section 1. Background for the procyclic computation (PLAN: "$H^1(\langle t\rangle,M)=\ker(\mathrm{Norm})/(t-1)M$").

**tate-cohomology** (definition). For finite $G$: $\hat H^n(G,M)=H^n(G,M)$ for $n\ge1$, $\hat H^0=M^G/N_GM$, $\hat H^{-1}=\ker N_G/I_GM$, $\hat H^{-n}=H_{n-1}(G,M)$ for
$n\ge2$. A short exact sequence of $G$-modules gives a long exact sequence of $\hat H^n$ in all degrees; $\#G$ kills every $\hat H^n(G,M)$; induced modules
$\mathbb{Z}[G]\otimes A$ have $\hat H^n=0$ for all $n$; for cyclic $G$, $\hat H^n\simeq\hat H^{n+2}$.
*Stated; reference.* Serre, Local Fields, Chapter VIII, Section 1; Brown, Chapter VI, Section 4. Background (the real place).

**z2-on-l-adic** (corollary). Let $\ell$ be odd, $G$ of order $2$ (e.g. $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})$ generated by complex conjugation) and $M$ a $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module with
$\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-linear $G$-action (type (D) $\ell$-primary, (C) or (V)). Then $H^n(G,M)=0$ for all $n\ge1$, $M=M^+\oplus M^-$ with $M^\pm=\{m:\sigma m=\pm m\}$, and
$H^0(G,M)=M^+$. For an arbitrary $G$-module $M$, $H^n(G,M)$ is killed by $2$; so $H^1(\mathbb{R},M):=H^1(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}),M)$ is a $2$-torsion group, zero
when $M$ is $\ell$-adic with $\ell$ odd.
*Proved here.* From order-kills. The paragraph after Lemma lem:dfg21 ("$H^1(\mathbb{R},T^D)$ is killed by $2$, so $H^1_f(\mathbb{R},T^D)=0$ for $\ell$ odd"); PLAN
"cohomology of the real place".

### Section 7: profinite groups and continuous cohomology

**discrete-g-module** (definition). Profinite group (recalled): compact Hausdorff totally disconnected, equivalently an inverse limit of finite groups; open normal
subgroups form a basis at $1$. A discrete $G$-module is an abelian group with the discrete topology and a continuous action, equivalently every stabiliser is open,
equivalently $M=\bigcup_UM^U$ over open normal $U$. Finite modules and $\ell$-primary torsion discrete modules (e.g. $W=T\otimes\mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell$) are the type (D) modules.
*Proved here.* Serre, Galois Cohomology, Chapter I, Sections 1 and 2; NSW, Chapter I, Sections 1 and 2. Background.

**continuous-cohomology** (definition / proposition). For $G$ profinite and $M$ a topological $G$-module, $C^n_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,M)=\{\varphi\colon G^n\to M\text{ continuous}\}$
is a subcomplex and $H^n(G,M):=H^n(C^\bullet_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,M))$. (D) For discrete $M$, $H^n(G,M)=\varinjlim_UH^n(G/U,M^U)$ over open normal $U$ with inflation as
transition maps (every continuous cochain is locally constant, factors through some $(G/U)^n$ and takes values in some $M^{U'}$). (C) For $M$ a finitely generated
$\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module with the $\ell$-adic topology, a continuous action is a continuous homomorphism $G\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}(M)$ (profinite topology on the target).
(V) $M=V$ a finite-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-vector space with continuous action. Convention: all cohomology of profinite groups in this book is continuous cochain
cohomology. $H^0(G,M)=M^G$; $H^1(G,M)=Z^1_{\mathrm{cont}}/B^1$; for trivial action $H^1(G,M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,M)$. For finite $G$ this is the cohomology of
inhomogeneous-cochains.
*Proved here (the direct limit statement in full).* Serre, Galois Cohomology, Chapter I, Section 2; NSW, Chapter I, Section 2 and Chapter II, Section 7; Tate95, Section 2.
Every $\ell$-adic cohomology group of the paper: $H^1(G,\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1))$, $H^1(I_q,\operatorname{ad}^0T)$, $H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,T^D)$, $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},W)$.

**les-continuous** (proposition). Let $G$ be profinite and $0\to M'\xrightarrow{i}M\xrightarrow{p}M''\to0$ exact with continuous $G$-maps, $i$ a homeomorphism onto its closed
image, and $p$ admitting a continuous section $s\colon M''\to M$ (continuous map with $ps=\operatorname{id}$, not necessarily additive). Then
$0\to C^\bullet_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,M')\to C^\bullet_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,M)\to C^\bullet_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,M'')\to0$ is exact and there is a natural long exact sequence
$\cdots\to H^n(G,M')\to H^n(G,M)\to H^n(G,M'')\xrightarrow{\delta}H^{n+1}(G,M')\to\cdots$. A continuous section exists in each case used in this book: (i) $M''$ discrete;
(ii) $p$ a surjection of finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-modules (lift generators; on a cyclic factor $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^a$ use the representatives $0,\dots,\ell^a-1\subset\mathbb{Z}_\ell$);
(iii) $0\to T\to V\to W\to0$ with $T$ of type (C) without $\ell$-torsion, $V=T\otimes\mathbb{Q}_\ell$, $W=V/T$; (iv) $0\to T\xrightarrow{\ell^k}T\to T/\ell^kT\to0$.
In case (iii) the connecting map $H^0(G,W)\to H^1(G,T)$ has kernel the image of $H^0(G,V)$; its image is the torsion of $H^1(G,T)$ (proved in Chapter 17).
*Proved here.* NSW, Chapter II, Section 7; Tate95, Section 2; Rubin, Euler Systems, Appendix B. Lemma lem:tamfactor, Lemma lem:dfg21, Lemma lem:h0 (the sequences
$T\to V\to W$ and $T\xrightarrow{\ell^k}T\to T/\ell^k$ of Chapters 17 and 18).

**tate-limits** (theorem, Tate). Let $G$ be profinite and $T=\varprojlim_kT_k$ an inverse limit of finite discrete $G$-modules with surjective transition maps, with the
inverse-limit topology (e.g. $T$ of type (C), $T_k=T/\ell^kT$). For every $n\ge0$ there is a natural exact sequence
$0\to\varprojlim{}^1_kH^{n-1}(G,T_k)\to H^n(G,T)\to\varprojlim_kH^n(G,T_k)\to0$. In particular (i) $H^1(G,T)=\varprojlim_kH^1(G,T_k)$ always (the $H^0(G,T_k)$ are finite,
so Mittag-Leffler holds); (ii) $H^n(G,T)=\varprojlim_kH^n(G,T_k)$ whenever all $H^{n-1}(G,T_k)$ are finite. Moreover $H^n(G,V)=H^n(G,T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ for
$V=T\otimes\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ (a continuous cochain into $V$ has compact image, hence lies in $\ell^{-a}T$).
*Stated; reference; the case $n=1$ sketched.* Tate95, Section 2; NSW, Chapter II, Section 7; Rubin, Euler Systems, Appendix B.
Lemma lem:tamfactor ("$H^1(G,\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1))=\varprojlim\mathbb{Q}_q^\times/(\mathbb{Q}_q^\times)^{\ell^k}$"), the definition of $H^1(I_q,\operatorname{ad}^0T)$.

**pitfalls** (warning). (i) For $M$ of type (C), $H^n(G,M)$ is not $\varinjlim_UH^n(G/U,M^U)$: $H^1(\mathbb{Z}_\ell,\mathbb{Z}_\ell)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_\ell,\mathbb{Z}_\ell)=\mathbb{Z}_\ell$,
whereas $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_\ell,\mathbb{Z}_\ell^{\mathrm{discrete}})=0$ (a continuous homomorphism from a compact group to a discrete torsion-free group is zero). (ii) The long exact
sequence needs the continuous section; it always exists in the cases listed. (iii) $\varprojlim^1$ terms can be nonzero in general; they vanish under finiteness.
(iv) $H^n(G,V)$ for $V$ of type (V) is a $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$-vector space and sees no torsion; the torsion of $H^1(G,T)$ is governed by $H^0(G,W)$ (Chapter 17). (v) For finite
$G$ (discrete topology) continuous and abstract cohomology coincide. (vi) For a procyclic group the formula $H^1=M/(F-1)M$ has precise hypotheses (Section 9); it fails
for finite cyclic groups ($\ker N/(F-1)M$) and for non-torsion discrete modules ($H^1(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbb{Z})=0$).
*Proved here (examples).* Tate95; Serre, Galois Cohomology, Chapter I. Background.

**h1-finitely-generated** (proposition). Let $G$ be profinite and $T$ of type (C). (i) $H^1(G,T)=\varprojlim_kH^1(G,T/\ell^kT)$ is a compact pro-$\ell$ $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module.
(ii) If $H^1(G,T/\ell T)$ is finite then $H^1(G,T)$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module, $H^1(G,V)=H^1(G,T)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ is finite-dimensional and
$H^1(G,T)_{\mathrm{tors}}$ is finite. (iii) The finiteness in (ii) holds for $G=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q/\mathbb{F}_q)$, $G=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R})$, $G=I_q$ and
$G=I_q/K$ with $\ell\ne q$ (proved here via the procyclic and prime-to-$\ell$ results), and for $G=G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$ and $G_{\mathbb{Q},S}$ (Chapter 17).
*(i) proved here; (ii) sketched (topological Nakayama); (iii) proved here for the listed groups except the last two.* Tate95, Section 2; Rubin, Appendix B.
Lemma lem:tamfactor ("$H^1(I,M)_{\mathrm{tor}}$ is $(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{v_\ell(n)})^2$"), paragraph after Lemma lem:dfg21 ("$H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,T^D)$ is the torsion of
$H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,T^D)$, a finite group").

### Section 8: groups of order prime to $\ell$ and pro-$\ell$ quotients

**supernatural-order-pro-l** (definition). The order of a profinite group $G$ is the supernatural number $\#G=\operatorname{lcm}_U\#(G/U)$ ($U$ open normal), a formal
product $\prod_pp^{n_p}$, $n_p\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}$. $G$ is pro-$\ell$ if $\#G$ is a power of $\ell$; $G$ has order prime to $\ell$ if $\ell\nmid\#G$. The maximal
pro-$\ell$ quotient $G^{(\ell)}=G/K$, $K$ the intersection of the open normal subgroups of $\ell$-power index: every continuous homomorphism from $G$ to a pro-$\ell$ group
factors through $G^{(\ell)}$; $G^{(\ell)}=1$ if $\ell\nmid\#G$. The unipotent upper triangular matrices in $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ form a pro-$\ell$ group isomorphic
to $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. Sylow pro-$\ell$ subgroups exist and are conjugate (stated).
*Stated; reference (elementary parts proved).* Serre, Galois Cohomology, Chapter I, Section 1; NSW, Chapter I, Section 1. Lemma lem:tamfactor ("trivial pro-$\ell$ quotient").

**prime-to-l-vanishing** (theorem). Let $G$ be profinite of order prime to $\ell$. (a) If $M$ is of type (D) and $\ell$-primary torsion then $H^n(G,M)=0$ for all $n\ge1$
(each $H^n(G/U,M^U)$ is killed by $\#(G/U)$ and by a power of $\ell$). (b) If $M$ is of type (C) or (V) then $H^n(G,M)=0$ for $n\ge1$ (from (a) and tate-limits).
(c) If $G$ acts trivially on $M$ of type (C) then $H^1(G,M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(G,M)=0$, and for this only $G^{(\ell)}=1$ is needed (the image of $G$ in the
pro-$\ell$ group $M$ is a pro-$\ell$ quotient of $G$). (d) In all cases $M^G$ is a direct summand of $M$.
*Proved here ((a), (c), (d) in full; (b) via tate-limits).* Serre, Galois Cohomology, Chapter I, Sections 2 and 3; NSW, Chapter I; Brown, Chapter III, Section 10.
Lemma lem:tamfactor ("the kernel of $I\to\langle t\rangle$ has trivial pro-$\ell$ quotient").

**cohomology-through-pro-l-quotient** (corollary). Let $G$ be profinite, $K\trianglelefteq G$ closed, and $M$ of type (D, $\ell$-primary), (C) or (V) with $K$ acting
trivially. (a) If $K^{(\ell)}=1$ then $\operatorname{inf}\colon H^1(G/K,M)\to H^1(G,M)$ is an isomorphism. (b) If $\ell\nmid\#K$ then $\operatorname{inf}\colon H^n(G/K,M)\to H^n(G,M)$
is an isomorphism for all $n$. (c) If the image of $G$ in $\operatorname{Aut}(M)$ is pro-$\ell$ (e.g. unipotent upper triangular in a basis of $M\simeq\mathbb{Z}_\ell^2$) then
the action factors through $G^{(\ell)}$, and $H^1(G,M)=H^1(G^{(\ell)},M)$.
*(a), (c) proved here; (b) stated (Hochschild-Serre).* As above. Lemma lem:tamfactor.

**tame-inertia-example** (example). Let $q$ be a prime, $I=I_q$ the inertia subgroup of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_q}$, $P\subset I$ the wild inertia (pro-$q$), $\ell\ne q$. Facts from
Chapter 7: $I/P\simeq\prod_{\ell'\ne q}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell'}(1)$; the maximal pro-$\ell$ quotient of $I$ is $I^{(\ell)}=\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$, the quotient map being the tame character
$t_\ell$; its kernel $K$ has order prime to $\ell$. Hence for every $M$ of type (C) on which $I$ acts with pro-$\ell$ image (e.g. through unipotent matrices), the action
factors through $\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)\simeq\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, topologically generated by an element $t$, and $H^1(I,M)=H^1(\langle t\rangle,M)=M/(t-1)M$ (procyclic-compact),
$H^n(I,M)=0$ for $n\ge2$.
*Proved here from the imported facts.* Serre, Local Fields, Chapter IV; Chapter 7 of this book. Lemma lem:tamfactor ("the inertia group acts through its maximal
pro-$\ell$ tame quotient, generated by $t$").

### Section 9: procyclic groups

**procyclic-groups** (definition / lemma). A profinite group is procyclic if topologically generated by one element; these are the quotients of
$\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}=\varprojlim\mathbb{Z}/n=\prod_p\mathbb{Z}_p$, i.e. the groups $\prod_p\mathbb{Z}/p^{a_p}$ ($a_p\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}$, $\mathbb{Z}/p^\infty:=\mathbb{Z}_p$). Examples:
$\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_q/\mathbb{F}_q)$ generated by Frobenius; $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$; $\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$; $I_q/P_q$. A continuous action of a procyclic $\Gamma$ with
generator $F$ on $M$ of type (C) is determined by $u=F|_M\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}(M)$: for $\Gamma=\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ every $u$ arises; for $\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ exactly the $u$ with
$u^{\ell^N}\to1$ as $N\to\infty$ (the closed subgroup generated by $u$ is pro-$\ell$), for instance every unipotent $u$.
*Proved here.* Serre, Galois Cohomology, Chapter I, Section 1; NSW, Chapter I, Section 1. Background; Lemma lem:tamfactor ($t$).

**cocycle-on-powers** (lemma). Let $\Gamma$ be procyclic with generator $F$, $M$ a Hausdorff topological $\Gamma$-module and $c$ a continuous $1$-cocycle. Then
$c(F^k)=(1+F+\dots+F^{k-1})c(F)$ for $k\ge1$, $c(F^{-k})=-F^{-k}c(F^k)$, so $c$ is determined by $c(F)$. If $M$ is of type (C), (V) or finite $\ell$-primary (so that
$(F-1)M$ is closed), then for every $a\in\Gamma$: $c(F^a)\equiv a\cdot c(F)\pmod{(F-1)M}$, where $a$ acts on the $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module $M/(F-1)M$ through the canonical map
$\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ (both sides are continuous in $a$ and agree on $\mathbb{Z}$, since $1+F+\dots+F^{k-1}\equiv k$ modulo $(F-1)$).
*Proved here.* Elementary. Lemma lem:tamfactor ("$c(t^k)\equiv k\,c(t)$ modulo $(t-1)M$", applied with $k=q^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_\ell$).

**procyclic-discrete** (theorem, Serre). Let $\Gamma=\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ with generator $F$ and $M$ a discrete torsion $\Gamma$-module, or $\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ and $M$ a discrete
$\ell$-primary torsion $\Gamma$-module. Then $H^0(\Gamma,M)=M^{F=1}$, $c\mapsto c(F)$ induces $H^1(\Gamma,M)\simeq M/(F-1)M$, and $H^n(\Gamma,M)=0$ for $n\ge2$. If $M$ is
finite then $\#H^1(\Gamma,M)=\#H^0(\Gamma,M)$. (Proof of $H^1$: $H^1(\Gamma,M)=\varinjlim_N\ker(N_{F,N})/(F-1)M^{\Gamma_N}$ over the finite quotients $\Gamma/\Gamma_N$, and every
$a\in M$ fixed by $\Gamma_N$ and of order $m$ satisfies $N_{F,Nm}(a)=m\,N_{F,N}(a)=0$.) Warning: false for finite cyclic $\Gamma$ and for non-torsion $M$.
*$H^1$ and the counting proved here; $H^n$, $n\ge2$, stated with reference.* Serre, Local Fields, Chapter XIII, Section 1; Serre, Galois Cohomology, Chapter I, Sections 3 and 4.
Chapter 17 ($H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}$ with finite coefficients, orders of unramified cohomology).

**procyclic-compact** (theorem; the identity the paper uses). Let $\Gamma$ be a procyclic profinite group admitting a continuous surjection onto $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ (in
$\Gamma\simeq\prod_p\mathbb{Z}/p^{a_p}$: $a_\ell=\infty$; examples $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, $\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$, $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$, $I_q/P_q$ for $q\ne\ell$), $F$ a topological generator, and $M$ of type
(C) or (V). Then: (i) $H^0(\Gamma,M)=\ker(F-1)$; (ii) every $m\in M$ is $c(F)$ for a unique continuous $1$-cocycle $c$ (the cocycle $k\mapsto(1+F+\dots+F^{k-1})m$ on $\mathbb{Z}$ is
uniformly continuous for the topology induced by $\Gamma$ and extends), and $c\mapsto c(F)$ induces an isomorphism of $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-modules $H^1(\Gamma,M)\simeq M/(F-1)M$;
(iii) $H^n(\Gamma,M)=0$ for $n\ge2$. Consequences: $H^1(\Gamma,M)$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, $H^1(\Gamma,M)_{\mathrm{tors}}$ is finite, and for $M=V$ of type (V),
$H^1(\Gamma,V)=V/(F-1)V=0$ if and only if $1$ is not an eigenvalue of $F$ on $V$, i.e. $\det(1-F\mid V)\ne0$. Ingredients of the proof of (ii): the image $\Gamma'$ of
$\Gamma$ in $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}(M)$ is $C\times\Gamma'_\ell$ with $C$ finite cyclic of order $m$ prime to $\ell$ and $\Gamma'_\ell$ pro-$\ell$ procyclic generated by
$u=F^m|_M$; for such $u$ ($u^{\ell^N}\to1$) and every $n$ there is $N$ with $(1+u+\dots+u^{j-1})M\subseteq\ell^nM$ whenever $\ell^N\mid j$ (write $u^{\ell^{N_0}}=1+\ell^nA$).
*(i), (ii) proved here in full; (iii) stated with reference.* For (iii): Serre, Galois Cohomology, Chapter I, Sections 3 and 4 ($\mathrm{cd}_\ell(\mathbb{Z}_\ell)=1$) and
Tate95, Section 2; for (ii) also Rubin, Appendix B. Lemma lem:tamfactor ("$H^1(\langle t\rangle,M')=M'/(t-1)M'$ for every $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module $M'$ of finite type with this
action"; "$H^1(G/I,\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)^I)=\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)/(q-1)$"), the paragraph after Lemma lem:dfg21 ($H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_p,V^D)=(V^D)^{I_p}/(\operatorname{Frob}_p-1)=0$),
Chapter 17 ($H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_p,T)=T^{I_p}/(\operatorname{Frob}_p-1)$).

**procyclic-examples** (example). (i) Trivial action: $H^1(\mathbb{Z}_\ell,M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathbb{Z}_\ell,M)=M$, and $H^1(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},M)=M$ via $\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{Z}_\ell$.
(ii) $\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$ with $F$ acting by an integer $q$ prime to $\ell$: $H^0=0$ if $q\ne1$ and $H^1(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1))=\mathbb{Z}_\ell/(q-1)\mathbb{Z}_\ell\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{v_\ell(q-1)}$;
with $\mu_{\ell^k}$: $\#H^1=\#H^0=\ell^{\min(k,v_\ell(q-1))}$. (iii) $M=\mathbb{Z}_\ell^2$ with $F=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&n'\\0&1\end{psmallmatrix}$: $H^1=\mathbb{Z}_\ell/n'\oplus\mathbb{Z}_\ell$,
torsion $\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{v_\ell(n')}$. (iv) $F$ of finite order prime to $\ell$: $M/(F-1)M=M_F$ is a direct summand, isomorphic to $M^F$. (v) $V$ of type (V):
$H^1(\Gamma,V)=0$ iff $\det(1-F\mid V)\ne0$.
*Proved here.* Elementary. Lemma lem:tamfactor, the paragraph after Lemma lem:dfg21, Chapter 17 (Kummer: $\mathbb{Q}_q^\times/(\mathbb{Q}_q^\times)^{\ell^k}$).

### Section 10: the model computation (the group cohomology of Lemma lem:tamfactor)

**model-setting** (construction / convention). Data: a profinite group $G$; a closed normal subgroup $I\trianglelefteq G$; a closed subgroup $K\subseteq I$, normal in $G$, of order
prime to $\ell$, with $I/K\simeq\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ topologically generated by $t$; an element $\operatorname{Frob}\in G$ whose image topologically generates $G/I\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$; a positive
integer $q$ prime to $\ell$ with $\operatorname{Frob}\,t\,\operatorname{Frob}^{-1}=t^q$ in $G/K$, equivalently $\operatorname{Frob}^{-1}t\operatorname{Frob}=t^{q^{-1}}$ with $q^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_\ell$;
a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module $M$ with a continuous $G$-action $\rho\colon G\to\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}(M)$ trivial on $K$. In the paper: $G=G_{\mathbb{Q}_q}$, $I=I_q$,
$K=\ker(t_\ell\colon I_q\to\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1))$, $t$ a generator of $\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)$, $\operatorname{Frob}$ a lift of the arithmetic Frobenius, and $M=\operatorname{ad}^0T_\ell E$ or $T_\ell E$ for $E$ with
multiplicative reduction at $q$ (the action of $I_q$ is unipotent, hence factors through $I_q^{(\ell)}$: Chapter 11); the relation is Chapter 7's.
*Convention; the imported facts are stated with pointers.* DFG04, p. 708; FPR94, I.4.2.2. Lemma lem:tamfactor, Section 1.2 of the paper.

**frobenius-on-h1-inertia** (proposition). In the model setting: (a) $\operatorname{inf}\colon H^1(I/K,M)\to H^1(I,M)$ is an isomorphism and $H^1(I,M)\simeq M/(t-1)M$ via $c\mapsto c(t)$.
(b) Under this identification the conjugation action of $G/I$ on $H^1(I,M)$ is $\overline{\operatorname{Frob}}\cdot\bar m=q^{-1}\rho(\operatorname{Frob})\bar m$; this is well defined because
$\rho(\operatorname{Frob})(t-1)M=(t^q-1)\rho(\operatorname{Frob})M\subseteq(t-1)M$, and independent of the lift $\operatorname{Frob}$. (Proof: $(\operatorname{Frob}\cdot c)(t)=\rho(\operatorname{Frob})c(\operatorname{Frob}^{-1}t\operatorname{Frob})
=\rho(\operatorname{Frob})c(t^{q^{-1}})\equiv q^{-1}\rho(\operatorname{Frob})c(t)$ by cocycle-on-powers.) (c) $H^1(I,M)_{\mathrm{tors}}=(M/(t-1)M)_{\mathrm{tors}}$ is finite and $G/I$-stable, and
$H^1(I,M)_{\mathrm{tors}}^{G/I}=\{\bar m\in(M/(t-1)M)_{\mathrm{tors}}:\rho(\operatorname{Frob})\bar m=q\bar m\}$.
*Proved here.* Combines conjugation-action, cohomology-through-pro-l-quotient and procyclic-compact. Lemma lem:tamfactor ("the arithmetic Frobenius acts on $M/(t-1)M$ as
$q^{-1}\rho(\operatorname{Frob})$").

**tate-curve-shape-matrices** (example). Let $T=\mathbb{Z}_\ell e_1\oplus\mathbb{Z}_\ell e_2$ with $\rho(g)=\begin{pmatrix}\chi(g)\varepsilon(g)&\kappa(g)\\0&\varepsilon(g)\end{pmatrix}$, where
$\varepsilon\colon G\to\{\pm1\}$ and $\chi\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$ are trivial on $I$ with $\chi(\operatorname{Frob})=q$, and $\kappa|_I\colon I\to\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is a continuous
homomorphism trivial on $K$ with $\kappa(t)=n'\ne0$, so $te_1=e_1$, $te_2=e_2+n'e_1$; write $\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\operatorname{Frob})$ and $c'=\kappa(\operatorname{Frob})$, so
$\rho(\operatorname{Frob})=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon q&c'\\0&\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}$. Let $M=\operatorname{ad}^0T$ (trace-zero endomorphisms, action $\varphi\mapsto\rho(g)\varphi\rho(g)^{-1}$) with
basis $e=E_{12}=e_1\otimes e_2^\vee$, $h=E_{11}-E_{22}$, $f=E_{21}=e_2\otimes e_1^\vee$. Since $\rho(t)=1+n'e$, $\rho(t)^{-1}=1-n'e$, $eh-he=-2e$, $ehe=0$, $ef-fe=h$, $efe=e$:
$(t-1)e=0$, $(t-1)h=-2n'e$, $(t-1)f=n'h-n'^2e$; and $\operatorname{Frob}\cdot e=qe$, $\operatorname{Frob}\cdot h=h-2\varepsilon c'e$, $\operatorname{Frob}\cdot f=q^{-1}(f+\varepsilon c'h-c'^2e)$.
The data $(n',c',\varepsilon)$ of the paper: $n'$ is $n=v_q(\Delta_E)$ times a unit (the restriction of the Kummer class to $I$ is $n$ times the tame character, Chapter 7),
$\varepsilon(\operatorname{Frob})=\pm1$ according as the reduction is split or nonsplit, and $c'$ is identified in Chapter 17.
*Proved here (verified symbolically).* Chapter 11 (Lemma lem:tamagawa-exp), DDT, Theorem 3.1(e), Silverman ATAEC, Chapter V for the origin of the shape.
Lemma lem:tamfactor (the basis $e,h,f$ and the three identities).

**model-computation** (proposition; the group-cohomological content of Lemma lem:tamfactor). Let $\ell$ be odd, $M=\operatorname{ad}^0T$ as in tate-curve-shape-matrices, $v=v_\ell(n')$.
(a) $(t-1)M=n'(\mathbb{Z}_\ell e\oplus\mathbb{Z}_\ell h)$ (because $2\in\mathbb{Z}_\ell^\times$), so $H^1(I,M)=M/(t-1)M=(\mathbb{Z}_\ell/n')\bar e\oplus(\mathbb{Z}_\ell/n')\bar h\oplus\mathbb{Z}_\ell\bar f$, with
$H^1(I,M)_{\mathrm{tors}}=(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^v)\bar e\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^v)\bar h\simeq(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^v)^2$. (b) $\overline{\operatorname{Frob}}$ acts on the torsion by $\bar e\mapsto\bar e$,
$\bar h\mapsto q^{-1}(\bar h-2\varepsilon c'\bar e)$. (c) $H^1(I,M)_{\mathrm{tors}}^{G/I}=\{x\bar e+y\bar h:(q-1)y\equiv0,\ c'y\equiv0\pmod{\ell^v}\}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/\ell^v\oplus\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m}$ with

$\ell^{\min(v,v_\ell(q-1))}\mathbb{Z}_\ell=(n',q-1)\mathbb{Z}_\ell$, which is exactly the ambiguity of $c'=\kappa(\operatorname{Frob})$ (changing the lift of $\operatorname{Frob}$ by $t^a$ changes $c'$ by $qn'a$,
changing $\kappa$ by a coboundary changes it by a multiple of $q-1$); the identification of this class with the unit part of the Tate parameter is Chapter 17's.

*Proved here, every step.* The paper's Lemma lem:tamfactor; FPR94, I.4.2.2; DFG04, p. 708. Lemma lem:tamfactor (all of it except the Kummer identification of $c'$ and the

**fitting-ideal-of-invariants** (corollary / remark). Recalled from Chapter 3: for a finite $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-module $A$, $\operatorname{Fitt}_{\mathbb{Z}_\ell}(A)=\ell^{\operatorname{length}A}\mathbb{Z}_\ell$,

### Section 11: cup products and Pontryagin duality

**cup-product** (definition / theorem). For $G$ (abstract; profinite with type (D) modules; or continuous cochains with type (C)) and $G$-modules $A$, $B$:
$(a\cup b)(g_1,\dots,g_{p+q})=a(g_1,\dots,g_p)\otimes g_1\cdots g_p\,b(g_{p+1},\dots,g_{p+q})\in A\otimes B$ satisfies $d(a\cup b)=da\cup b+(-1)^pa\cup db$ and induces
$H^p(G,A)\otimes H^q(G,B)\to H^{p+q}(G,A\otimes B)$; a $G$-pairing $A\times B\to C$ gives $H^p(G,A)\times H^q(G,B)\to H^{p+q}(G,C)$. Properties: bilinear, associative,
graded commutative ($a\cup b=(-1)^{pq}\tau_*(b\cup a)$), compatible with $\operatorname{res}$ and $\operatorname{inf}$, with connecting homomorphisms, and $\operatorname{cor}(\operatorname{res}a\cup b)=a\cup\operatorname{cor}b$.
For $p=q=1$: $(a\cup b)(g,h)=a(g)\otimes g\,b(h)$.
*Definition and the Leibniz formula in degrees $\le2$ proved here; the properties stated.* NSW, Chapter I, Section 4; Brown, Chapter V, Section 3; Serre, Local Fields,
Chapter VIII, Section 3; Cassels-Frohlich, Chapter IV. Chapter 17 (the pairing of Tate local duality $H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,M)\times H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,M^*(1))\to H^2(\mathbb{Q}_p,\mu)$, behind the
dual terms of Lemma lem:dfg21).

**pontryagin-duality-cohomology** (proposition / statement). Recalled from Chapter 3: $A^*=\operatorname{Hom}(A,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})$ for finite $A$ ($=\operatorname{Hom}(A,\mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ if
$A$ is $\ell$-primary); $T^*=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(T,\mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell)$ for $T$ of type (C) is discrete $\ell$-primary ($T$ free of rank $r$ gives $(\mathbb{Q}_\ell/\mathbb{Z}_\ell)^r$),
and $W^*$ for $W$ discrete $\ell$-primary is of type (C); $(-)^*$ is exact and $A^{**}=A$; lengths of finite modules are preserved; for a $G$-module $A$ the dual carries
$(g\varphi)(a)=\varphi(g^{-1}a)$ and is a $G$-module of the dual type. Statement: for $G$ profinite and $A$ a discrete torsion $G$-module, $H^n(G,A)^*\simeq H_n(G,A^*)$
(homology of profinite groups); for $G$ finite, $\hat H^n(G,A)^*\simeq\hat H^{-n-1}(G,A^*)$ for finite $A$. The duality the paper actually uses, Tate local duality,
is Chapter 17's theorem.
*Recalled / stated; reference.* Chapter 3; NSW, Chapter I; Brown, Chapter VI. Lemma lem:dfg21 (the Pontryagin duals $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,T^D)^*$, $H^0(\mathbb{Q},W^D)^*$).

**lengths-in-exact-sequences** (lemma). For finite $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-modules: $\operatorname{length}(A^*)=\operatorname{length}(A)$; dualising an exact sequence gives an exact sequence with
reversed arrows; in an exact sequence $0\to A\to B\to C\to D\to0$ of finite $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-modules, $\operatorname{length}B=\operatorname{length}A+\operatorname{length}C-\operatorname{length}D$.
Hence, in the exact sequence of Lemma lem:dfg21 with all terms finite,
$\operatorname{length}H^1_\Sigma(\mathbb{Q},W)=\operatorname{length}H^1_f(\mathbb{Q},W)+\sum_{p\in\Sigma\cup\{\infty\}}\operatorname{length}H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,T^D)-\operatorname{length}H^0(\mathbb{Q},W^D)$, the term at
$\infty$ being $0$ for $\ell$ odd.
*Proved here.* Chapter 3. Proof of Theorem thm:main ("$\operatorname{len}H^1_\Sigma=\operatorname{len}H^1_f+\sum\operatorname{len}H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,T^D)$"), Remark rem:ns (the corrected identity
with $-h_\ell(E)$).

### Section 12: Galois cohomology, notation

**galois-cohomology-convention** (convention). For a field $K$ with separable closure $\overline K$, $G_K=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline K/K)$ is profinite (Chapter 5); a Galois module is a
$G_K$-module of type (D), (C) or (V); $H^n(K,M):=H^n(G_K,M)$ (continuous cochains). For $K=\mathbb{Q}_p$: $I_p\trianglelefteq G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}$, $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}/I_p=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p/\mathbb{F}_p)\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$
generated by the arithmetic Frobenius $\operatorname{Frob}_p$ ($x\mapsto x^p$); $H^n(I_p,M)$ carries the conjugation action of $G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}/I_p$; $H^n(\mathbb{R},M):=H^n(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}),M)$;
$\mathbb{Z}_\ell(1)=\varprojlim\mu_{\ell^k}$ with $\chi_\ell(\operatorname{Frob}_p)=p$ for $p\ne\ell$; $\operatorname{Frob}_p$ never denotes the geometric Frobenius $\operatorname{Frob}_p^{-1}$. The unramified
part $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_p,M):=H^1(G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}/I_p,M^{I_p})=\ker(\operatorname{res}\colon H^1(\mathbb{Q}_p,M)\to H^1(I_p,M))$ (inflation-restriction), studied in Chapter 17.
*Convention (the equality of the two descriptions of $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}$ proved here).* Serre, Galois Cohomology, Chapter II, Section 1; NSW, Chapter VII. Sections 1.1 and 1.2
of the paper, Lemma lem:tamfactor, Lemma lem:dfg21 and the paragraph after it.

**unramified-h1-preview** (example). With $\Gamma=G_{\mathbb{Q}_p}/I_p\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $M$ of type (C): $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_p,M)=M^{I_p}/(\operatorname{Frob}_p-1)M^{I_p}$
(procyclic-compact applied to the closed submodule $M^{I_p}$); for $V$ of type (V): $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_p,V)=V^{I_p}/(\operatorname{Frob}_p-1)V^{I_p}=0$ if and only if
$\det(1-\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid V^{I_p})\ne0$. This is the paper's argument that $H^1_f(\mathbb{Q}_p,V^D)=H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_p,V^D)$ vanishes for $p\in\Sigma$, because
$\det(1-\operatorname{Frob}_p\mid(V^D)^{I_p})=L_p(A_f,1)^{-1}\ne0$ (the equality $H^1_f=H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}$ at $p\ne\ell$ and the value of the determinant are Chapters 18 and 19).
*Proved here.* Serre, Local Fields, Chapter XIII, Section 1; Rubin, Euler Systems, Section 1.3. The paragraph after Lemma lem:dfg21.

## 4. Notes for the writer

Conventions to fix and keep (they must agree with Chapters 7, 17, 18):

1. Conjugation action: $(\sigma\cdot c)(h)=\sigma\,c(\sigma^{-1}h\sigma)$, verbatim the paper's formula. With $\operatorname{Frob}^{-1}t\operatorname{Frob}=t^{q^{-1}}$ this gives the factor
   $q^{-1}$ in "$\overline{\operatorname{Frob}}$ acts on $M/(t-1)M$ as $q^{-1}\rho(\operatorname{Frob})$". Do not switch to the other common convention $c\mapsto\sigma^{-1}c(\sigma h\sigma^{-1})$
   (it gives $q\rho(\operatorname{Frob})^{-1}$; the invariants are the same, the formulas are not).
2. Frobenius is arithmetic throughout; the relation is $\operatorname{Frob}\,t\,\operatorname{Frob}^{-1}=t^q$ ("Frobenius raises tame inertia to the $q$-th power"); state it as an imported fact
   from Chapter 7 in exactly the two equivalent forms, and note $q^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ because $q\ne\ell$.
3. Well-definedness of the Frobenius action on $M/(t-1)M$ needs only $\rho(\operatorname{Frob})(t-1)M\subseteq(t-1)M$, which follows from $(t^q-1)=(t-1)(1+\dots+t^{q-1})$.
   Do not claim $(t^q-1)M=(t-1)M$ (true, but it needs $t-1$ in the radical of $\mathbb{Z}_\ell[t]$; unnecessary).
4. The identity $H^1(\Gamma,M)=M/(F-1)M$ has exact hypotheses: $\Gamma$ procyclic with a surjection onto $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ and $M$ of type (C) or (V); or $\Gamma=\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}$ and $M$ discrete
   torsion; or $\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ and $M$ discrete $\ell$-primary. It fails for finite cyclic $\Gamma$ ($\ker N/(F-1)M$), for $\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_p$, $p\ne\ell$, on $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-modules
   (again $\ker N/(F-1)M$), and for discrete non-torsion $M$ ($H^1(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbb{Z})=0$). Say so in a warning; Exercise 15 covers the $\mathbb{Z}_p$ case.
5. The lemma $c(t^a)\equiv a\,c(t)\pmod{(t-1)M}$ for $a\in\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ is the precise form of the paper's "$c(t^k)\equiv k\,c(t)$"; it needs $(t-1)M$ closed, which holds because
   finitely generated submodules of finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$-modules are closed (Chapter 3).
6. "The kernel of $I\to\langle t\rangle$ has trivial pro-$\ell$ quotient" (paper) is weaker than "has order prime to $\ell$"; both hold for tame inertia. For $H^1$ with trivial
   action the weaker property suffices (Hom$_{\mathrm{cont}}(K,M)=0$), and only the three-term inflation-restriction sequence is needed; no spectral sequence.
